screen  Assinatura answers what am I paying for, and lets a person change or stop it. Most people who open this surface after buying land here rather than on the pitch, so it is written as the primary billing screen and not as an appendix to one.

states  Stripe, Play, trial, lifetime, canceled, past due, lapsed, portal opening, portal failed, offline, load failed, loading. Hover, focus-visible and press are live. This screen carries no plan axis of its own, because the plan situation IS its state axis: this document and Orbit Pro are the two halves of one route, upgrade/page.tsx:95 shows the pitch when the account is not Pro and :123 shows this dashboard when it is, so a free account lands on the pitch and never here.

two paths  the Stripe and Play variants are structurally different, not cosmetically. A subscription bought through Google Play cannot open the Stripe portal, so the Play variant sends the person to the Play dashboard instead, with its own line naming where the subscription lives and why the change happens there. Nothing in the Play variant offers a portal, and nothing in the Stripe variant mentions Play. Drawing one and relabelling it would ship a button that cannot work.

portal  the Stripe path has its own two states, because opening a hosted portal is a request that can fail on its own: opening keeps the screen and puts the action in its loading state, and a failure states what happened, states the fix and offers a retry rather than a dead end. That failure is the portal itself not opening, which is a different thing from the subscription failing to load, and it is drawn separately.

billing  the billing dashboard the app ships is drawn in the app, not pointed at the provider. The invoice history (billing-dashboard.tsx:190-238) is a title and one row per invoice, each stating its reason from the four the code produces (:22-25, subscription created, subscription cycle, subscription updated, manual) and its status from the three (:32-34, paid, open, void), with the download as ListRow’s real per row action (:237-238). The earlier line here that sent people to the provider for receipts was false, the third false absence this run, and it is deleted. The payment method (:133-159) states the card brand, its last digits and its expiry, with the change action handing off to the portal like every other billing change. The usage figure (usage-stats.tsx) shows the thing the subscription buys, AI messages against the ceiling, with its bar. A billing LOAD failure (:81-89) is its own state with its own retry, a different thing from the portal failing to open, and both are drawn.

lifetime  read only, and it says so: no portal, no cancel, no interval, and no action at all, because there is nothing to change and no screen in the product can sell one. It states what it is and stops.

lapsed  the live defect: a lapsed plan currently reads as free with no account of what changed, so a person is left to work out why the calendar stopped syncing. Here it says the subscription ended, names the day it ended, and lists what is no longer on, in the same neutral tone as everything else. It carries one action to subscribe again, which leaves for Orbit Pro, the pitch half of this route, and no alarm colour, because lapsing is not an error. The reason it ended is decided (2026-08-20): named whenever it is actionable, phrased as a circumstance and never as blame, so a declined card is told to the person who can fix it; when the reason is not actionable the line is withheld entirely and the screen says only that the subscription ended. The reason line is drawn here carrying data-mock, since no endpoint produces it yet.

canceled  canceled and past due are real badged states (billing-dashboard.tsx:105, :108) and neither is lapsed: a canceled plan is still running and will not renew, so its line says when access ends (:115) while every other paying state says when it renews (:116); a past due plan keeps running while the provider retries, and the line says updating the card in the portal fixes it, a circumstance and never blame. Both keep the portal action, no alarm colour on either.

trial  a trial reads as plan pro with a separate trial flag, so the badge is derived from the two together and never read off the plan field, which says only pro or free. The badge states the trial and the facts state the day count. Nothing offers to start, extend or restart one, because nothing can.

hand off  billing is a hand off and not a step up, because the Stripe portal authenticates the person itself, so a code before opening it buys no security; the product's two step up operations stay account deletion and API keys. No card field, no address field and no credential appears anywhere in this document; the note at the end says the change happens with the provider. That is also why the action is a single button rather than a form.

offline  mobile renders offline on this screen, so the action is disabled with its reason beside it rather than failing on tap. The plan facts stay visible, because they were already loaded; only the hand off needs the network.

prices  no price is typed. The amount and interval on the card come from the same pricing response the pitch uses, and the renewal date comes from the subscription. Both are fixtures in the logic, labelled as standing in for the response.

spacing  4 8 12 16 24 32, gap only. Card padding 24, well padding 16, the action at the 50 button height.

radius  12 on wells, 20 on the plan card, 28 on the shell, 999 on buttons and the badge is 8.

accent  at most one filled action per state, and several states have none: lifetime has no action at all, and the Play variant's single action is the one filled thing on the screen. The badge is the neutral well rather than an accent chip, since a badge sits on a card and accent text is canvas only. Nothing here marks completion.

contrast  white on primary 4.57:1 rest, 5.11:1 hover, 6.09:1 pressed. No status colour appears: a lapsed plan and a failed portal are both boundaries rather than errors, so both use neutral tokens and say what to do.

mock  every number carries data-mock on the element that renders it: the amount, the renewal date, the trial day count, the purchase date, the card’s last digits and expiry, the usage figures, and every invoice’s date and amount. The lapsed reason line keeps its data-mock, since no endpoint produces it yet (api #344).

tests  1 no state offers an action the payment provider cannot perform. 2 the trial badge is derived from the plan and the flag together. 3 no credential is collected and no price is typed.

gaps  the mirrored bundle was checked before use, as every round since it first went stale: it already carried ListRow’s action, so nothing was re-synced and the invoice download rides the real per row button. 1 PlanCard is a selectable purchase card, so the current plan is composed rather than reusing it: this card is a statement, not a choice. 2 there is no store or provider row in the system, so the Play and Stripe lines are composed from a well and a glyph, and the payment method row the same way. 3 ErrorState centres its message and takes one action, which suits the failed LOAD, now drawn with it, but not an inline portal failure, so that one stays composed beside the action it belongs to.

decided  1 the lapsed reason is named whenever it is actionable, as a circumstance and never as blame, following the standing rule that copy names the circumstance, never the person; when it is not actionable the line is withheld entirely. 2 the Play variant deep links to the specific subscription, not the dashboard, on the condition that the implementing ticket confirms the URL shape against Google's own documentation rather than assuming it: never assuming an external interface is a standing rule, and a guessed deep link fails silently in the person's hands.

needs api  the lapsed reason has no source today: HandleWebhookCommand.cs:207-210 runs the same CancelStripeSubscription() for canceled and unpaid, User.cs:249-254 nulls the subscription id and clears the entitlement and stores nothing else, SubscriptionStatusResponse (SubscriptionDtos.cs:7-18) carries no reason field, and no invoice.payment_failed handler exists in the repository. Ticket #344 on the api carries it; the reason line here renders mock data so the shape is ready the moment the field exists.
