screen  Onboarding produces one real habit that the person typed, and ends with them looking at it. It is not a tour, not a quiz and not a preference survey: the tour and the push prompt system are deleted, so nothing here explains a feature the person has not asked about. The feature guide is not this screen's to claim: it lives on the About screen (about/page.tsx:84) and Sobre draws it.

entrances  the same flow mounts in two places. Signed in, layout.tsx:380 mounts it as an overlay over the app for an account that never finished, and it ends on Hoje. Signed out, onboarding/page.tsx:13 mounts it with isLive false: every answer is buffered on the device (onboarding-actions-context.tsx:96 and :133), nothing is sent to a server, the last step's words become the save your plan words with the action changed to match (onboarding-complete.tsx:102-109 and :159), the route goes to sign in (onboarding-actions-context.tsx:134), and the buffered habit is written to the account after sign in (use-onboarding-flush.ts:38). The first step carries the I already have an account link on that path only (onboarding-welcome.tsx:141-145).

position  one indicator: the mono counter the app draws (onboarding-flow.tsx:248-264), Orbit · 01 / 03, zero padded and tabular, the current number at fg-1 and the rest at fg-3, over THREE steps rather than the app's eight because three is what this flow has. An sr-only progress element travels with it (:290-296) so a screen reader gets the same position. The app also draws dots (:289); one indicator is enough, so the counter wins and the dots are not drawn. Skip goes straight to the end, so a person who does not want to type a habit right now is never trapped on the first screen.

decisions  three, and no more: what you want to keep doing, when it happens, and whether we may remind you. The first is typed, the second is proposed from what was typed and corrected by tapping, and the third is a yes or a no. Nothing else is collected, because everything else can be changed later from the habit itself.

input  the system field, not a composed pair: Input with multiline, three rows, and the words the parser consumed as marks with their required marksLabel, so mirror and textarea are one box from one declaration (the D71 drift defect) and the marked words carry an accessible name. maxLength rides the field because the app caps the title (MAX_HABIT_TITLE_LENGTH, onboarding-create-habit.tsx:182), drawn here at 100 with no counter: the limit stops the typing, it does not annotate the field. The quick start chips fill that field rather than skipping it, so the habit is still the person's own sentence.

permission  the ask comes after the habit exists, never before, and it shows the notification as it will actually arrive rather than describing one: the title is the habit's own title and the body is the relative time, Em 15 minutos and Due in 15 minutes, Agora and Due now at zero (ReminderSchedulerService.cs:142, :149, :322-338), timestamped at the moment it fires, the due time minus the default ReminderTimes of [15] (Habit.cs:77 and :159), so a habit due at 18:00 previews at 17:45 and never at 18:00, which is what the old bell row falsely showed. One quiet line under it states the two scheduling facts: it arrives 15 minutes before the habit, and it is not sent if the habit is already logged (:129-130). A habit left with NO time gets no ask at all: ProcessRelativeReminders walks ReminderTimes against habit.DueTime and does nothing without one, and onboarding never sets a fixed clock time (ScheduledReminderWhen exists but is not collected here), so the step says plainly that reminders need a time, offers to go back and set one, and its forward action continues without asking, because a permission the flow cannot use is never requested. In the two failure shapes and the no time shape the allow control is absent rather than dead.
